Jumper Crab Robot Challenge · round 2 and beyond

What wins next: one crab that turns out to be a thousand

Research says our lane is wide open. Nobody else is doing swarms, formations or prompt-driven shows, and the biggest rival group is 6–8 crabs. Overnight experiments say the boldest idea is buildable: 1,008 simulated crabs can pass for one robot until the camera dives in.

Updated 28 Oct 2026Round 1 post: v5 (ready)Code: dev branch · videos: deliverables branch

What the research found

The competition (about 25 entries on GitHub)

  • Crowded: single-crab dance styles (tango, ballet, b-boy, Crab Rave moves), skins, household-chore demos, RL sumo and soccer, browser games, cinematic Blender shorts.
  • Open: big synchronised swarms, formations that draw shapes and words, a full rave or stage show, and visible "prompt → show" AI. Every entry so far is sim-only.
  • None of the competitors show visible engagement yet; all have 0–1 stars.
  • The organisers pitch the toolkit as "describe it in one sentence and AI builds it", so our prompt-on-screen format matches their own story.

What goes viral, and what experts trust

  • Hook in the first 1.5 s with the most impossible frame. Loop the ending back to the start.
  • Scale reads instantly (drone-show records, Kilobots). Cuteness wins: the original Jumper went viral because it danced on the beat and looked happy.
  • Fails are gold, and real physics keeps them credible. Participation ("comment a word") compounds the audience score.
  • Experts trust per-crab variation, real contacts, robustness under pushes and stats, and an on-screen badge like 1.0× · MuJoCo · RL policy. Identical motion everywhere reads as animation.
  • Length: 16–25 s for Shorts and Douyin, 30–45 s on X. Douyin co-organises the contest, so post there too if you can.

The ideas, ranked

#1

“It’s just one crab.”

mosaic provenzoom provenround-2 hero · ~2 days

Open on what looks like a single Jumper robot. Its eyes come online, blink, and turn happy. The camera dives in… and it’s made of a thousand tiny crabs. One of them starts dancing, then they all do.

  1. 0:00A still, low-res “photo” of one robot crab in the dark. Hold it 1 s so it reads as a single robot.
  2. 0:01The face display flickers on: the pixel eyes light up, blink, then curve into happy ^ ^ eyes.
  3. 0:03A powers-of-ten dive into the crab’s eye. The eye is a crab, and its own little eyes blink back at the camera.
  4. 0:06That one crab starts dancing alone. The drop lands: zoom out to the whole field as the picture “breaks” and every crab jumps.
  5. 0:09Marching-band routines, but more fun: pinwheels, counter-marching lines, the giant crab waving its claws, a word, a jump party.
  6. 0:20Everyone runs back into place and the image reassembles into the single crab, which winks. The video loops to its first frame.

Why it wins

  • The reveal is a perfect hook and an instant loop.
  • “1,000 robots” reads in one frame, and the happy eyes add cuteness.
  • Experts see a thousand independent physics sims coordinating, not a VFX trick.

What it takes

  • The instanced crowd renderer is built. 2,500 crabs take about 24 s per frame, so a full video is about 1 hour on 24 cloud workers.
  • Still to do: high-detail models for the few crabs near the camera, splitting the simulation across workers, and the pixel-eye animation.
#2

Halloween jack-o’-lantern

formation provenpost 31 Oct · half a day

“It’s Halloween. Become a jack-o’-lantern.” 228 crabs light up orange, walk into a pumpkin with a glowing carved face, then the face “screams” as the crabs scatter in a jump party.

Why it wins

  • Topical posts get a boost on the day, and it shows the prompt works for any shape, not only words.
  • Cheap: the same pipeline, and about 30 minutes on the workers.

Extensions

  • The pumpkin face blinks, the crabs flee into “BOO”, and a ghost made of white crabs drifts across.
  • Use the same trick for any holiday or meme: a turkey for Thanksgiving, a snowflake in December.
#3

“Comment a word, the crabs spell it”

pipeline builtongoing · ~30 min per reply

Reply to commenters with a video of the swarm spelling their word. Their comment appears on screen as the prompt.

  • It feeds the 60% audience score directly. Every reply video is a fresh post, and commenters share their own.
  • The prompt parser already turns plain sentences into plans (“glow blue, form a spiral, jump on the drop, then spell X” rendered end to end). Short words read best: two or three letters, or four with more crabs.

#6

Later rounds: a crab you can direct live

bigger buildround 3

A web page where anyone types a prompt and a small swarm performs it right there in the browser. Jumper’s controller already ships a browser build. This scores highest on “useful”, gives every post a link people can try, and is the strongest pitch for experts.

Small things that lift every video

  • Name one hero crab and bring it back in every video (it is the one the camera dives into).
  • Keep the corner badge 1.0× · MuJoCo · trained RL policy · no keyframes on screen, and add bilingual EN/中文 captions.
  • Open on the most impossible frame, cut the Shorts/Douyin version to 16–25 s, and end on the first frame so it loops.
